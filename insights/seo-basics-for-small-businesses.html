SEO basics for small businesses: where to start

Search engine optimisation can feel complicated when you're starting out. The best first step is to make your website clear, useful and technically accessible — then measure how people discover it.

By Bashademy•2026-11-27

1. Make sure your website can be found

Start by checking whether your important pages are accessible to search engines. Make sure your website is published, important pages are not accidentally blocked, and visitors can navigate between related pages using ordinary links.

2. Give every important page a clear purpose

A service page should explain one service in enough detail to help a potential customer decide what to do next. Use a descriptive page title, one clear main heading, useful subheadings and a concise description of the page.

3. Write for the questions customers actually ask

Think about the problems, comparisons and decisions that lead someone to your business. Create content that answers those questions with specific, original information. Avoid repeating the same phrase unnaturally or creating pages that differ only by a few keywords.

4. Build a sensible internal-link structure

Link from your main services page to individual service pages. Link relevant articles to those services when the connection genuinely helps the reader. Use descriptive link text so visitors know what they will find.

5. Measure what happens

Google Search Console can show which queries generate impressions and clicks, which pages appear in search and whether technical issues are reported. Analytics can help you understand what visitors do after they arrive. Use these tools to decide what to improve next.

A simple starting checklist

  • Confirm your website is indexed and important pages are crawlable.
  • Write a unique title and meta description for each key page.
  • Make your service pages useful and specific.
  • Ensure the site works well on mobile.
  • Connect Google Search Console and submit your sitemap.
  • Review performance regularly and update content when needed.
A practical next step

Review your current website and choose one improvement you can implement and measure. Consistent, useful improvements are more valuable than chasing quick fixes.
